Between stacks

Bridges: chains that talk

One chain cannot read another's records. A bridge passes messages and tokens between them, and how it is built decides who you have to trust.

Trusted bridges

The simplest bridge is a group of operators who watch one chain and sign messages on the other. Tokens are usually locked on one side and an IOU is issued on the other. It works, but you trust the operators: if their keys are stolen, the locked tokens can be taken. Some of the largest thefts in crypto have been bridge thefts.

Light-client bridges: IBC

Cosmos chains use IBC, the Inter-Blockchain Communication protocol. Each chain runs a light client of the other: a small program that follows the other chain's block headers and checks its validators' signatures. A relayer, which anyone can run, carries messages and their proofs; the receiving chain checks each proof itself, so the relayer needs no trust.

Connected chains stay separate. Each keeps its own validators and rules, and they agree only on how to pass messages. Some writers call this connecting layer “layer 0”; the term has no single meaning.
