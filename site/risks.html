Risks and limits

The same features that remove the middleman remove the safety net. What goes wrong, and why.

In one lineNo middleman means no safety net: mistakes and thefts are usually final.

01You are the bank

  • Lost keys: there is no reset; the funds stay where they are, out of reach.
  • Phishing and fake apps: a site that gets you to sign a transaction can empty a wallet, and a confirmed transaction cannot be reversed.
  • Wrong address: a payment to a mistyped address is gone.

02Code and bridges

  • Contract bugs: a contract does exactly what its code says, including its mistakes, and anyone may call it.
  • Bridges hold large pools of locked tokens, which makes them targets.

03The network itself

  • Majority attacks: whoever controls most of the mining power (proof of work) can reorder recent blocks; on a BFT chain, a third of the stake can stop the chain by refusing to vote, and more than a third voting twice can finalize conflicting blocks.
  • Energy: proof of work uses a great deal of electricity by design. Ethereum's move to proof of stake cut its energy use by about 99.95%, by its own estimate.
  • Speed and cost: a chain everyone can check is slower and dearer per transaction than one company's database. Many uses do not need a blockchain at all.
