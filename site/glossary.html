21 terms, in plain words

Glossary

ABFGHLMNPRSVW

A

Address
Where something is sent: a shortened hash of a public key, or a contract's location.

B

Block
A batch of transactions, with a header that includes the previous block's hash.
Bridge
A system that passes messages or tokens between two chains.

F

Finality
The point after which a block cannot be reversed. In BFT chains, once more than two thirds of the voting power sign it.
Fork
Two competing versions of a chain's history. Proof-of-work chains wait for one to win; BFT chains cannot produce one while fewer than a third of the voting power is faulty.

G

Gas
The unit that measures computation and storage on Ethereum and similar chains. The fee is the gas used times a price per unit.

H

Hash
A fixed-length fingerprint of some data, here SHA-256's 64 hexadecimal digits (0–9 and a–f). Finding two inputs with the same hash is infeasible.

L

Layer 1
A main chain, such as Bitcoin or Ethereum, that settles transactions itself.
Layer 2
A system that runs transactions off a layer 1 and settles them on it, such as a rollup or payment channel.
Light client
A small program that follows another chain's headers and signatures, so it can check proofs from it.

M

Merkle root
One hash that commits to a whole list of transactions, so a short proof can show that one is in the list.

N

Nonce
In proof of work, the number miners change until the block's hash meets the target.

P

Private key
The secret number that signs your transactions. Whoever has it controls the funds.
Proof of stake
Consensus where validators lock up tokens as a deposit and vote on blocks.
Proof of work
Consensus where adding a block requires finding a hash that meets a target, by guessing.

R

Recovery phrase
12 or 24 words that can recreate a wallet's keys. Whoever has the phrase has the funds.
Rollup
A layer 2 that posts batches of transactions, and either a proof or a challenge period, to a layer 1.

S

Smart contract
A program stored on the chain, run by every node with the same result.
Stake
Tokens a validator locks up as a deposit. Its voting power grows with its stake, and it can lose some for breaking the rules.

V

Validator
A computer that takes part in consensus by proposing and voting on blocks.

W

Wallet
Software that keeps your private keys and signs transactions with them.
